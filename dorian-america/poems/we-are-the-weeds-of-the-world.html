We are the weeds of the world

We have no pedigrees, we do not have shapely leaves or lavender flowers. We do not have classic features. We appear where we are not wanted and are an embarrassment to those who appreciate beauty and sip aperitifs in the late afternoon. We appear in the cracks in sidewalks and sometimes if allowed to be there too long we can crack the pavements of civilized societies. We grow in garbage dumps, in sewer ventilators, in the urinals of the rich, our thorny vines crawl from bidets across dressing tables toward fluorescent lights buried in black ceilings. We are ragged and unkempt but we are suited to wild storms offshore that flay our leaves with wind and wet sand, that would tear hothouse flowers to shreds. We can endure earthquakes because we are not in flower pots. We know gulls and geese and gray seas at dawn, we watch seals past the surf at daybreak, we thirst for water so our roots sink deeper in the dunes. We are the weeds of the world, native in every lush or forbidding place, without attention from sprinkling cans or compost spreaders. We cling to alpine crags and pry loose great boulders to fall each century changing forever the face of the earth, a power unknown to roses and daffodils. The weeds of the world can cover wartime's landscapes with soft green, can cover tombs of generals and dictators with dark green vines that call to insects with tiny flowers, to renew it all again - without end.. We are the weeds of the world.
