A Clothesline Between Galaxies

I know. It's never been done before. But if an ordinary rope was spun as fine as the finest silky spider web it might reach. Let's just say it reaches. We'll attach it here and the other end on some kind of a stick on some small planet in Andromeda where there's life - at least where there's a stick. Yet that great depth of space - 2,000 000 light years and yet Andromeda is one of the closest and it looks tiny even though it's 150,000 light years on its long diameter. Even leaving here we would feel forlorn and out of breath even before we left our solar system. Nothingness, nothingness on every side. And remember, a light year is a unit of distance, not time. Travelling at 186,000 miles per second, it's the distance light travels in one year; or it's 186,000 x 60 seconds x 60 minutes x 24 hours x 365 days, or 586 billion miles and change; then multiply by the number of light years to Andromeda and you get 11.7 million trillion miles and more change, and that tells you how long our clothesline needs to be. But why? It is to be remembered by a universe that creates us from the silence of space to let us coalesce into suns and planets and seas and shores and lakes and trees and creatures that swim then crawl then climb then fly then walk, then read, then use typewriters; with lungs and hearts and limbs and . . . what for? To live then die and vanish? Is that the way the universe looks at us? I'm afraid so. You see, I don't think it really cares. I don't think it keeps score no matter what Moses and the apostle Paul and Allah tell us. I don't think the universe ever heard of us. It's a little too big and there are so many records to keep, or would be. So a clothesline is some kind of a cry to say. "Hey, look at us! See what we did! Look at the drawings I made as a child, we each made as children. I want to tell you about the time I first fell in love, the first time we all fell in love. I want to hang a Xerox copy of my first paycheck on the clothesline, the birth certificate of each of my children, a description of my wife's underwear and how much I like her when she's wearing the lavendar ones or none at all." Have you any idea universe, how wonderful this is? You know, I think the answer is "no". You don't know. You don't even understand what I'm saying. Mostly you don't even hear me - you're too far away. I want to hang a tape recorder that plays endlessly the music played at my grandmother's funeral. I want you to look at a copy of an old tintype of an uncle of mine 5 generations back standing there in his Civil War uniform his union army cap and coat, holding a rifle beside him, the butt on the ground. The Civil War - yes, so many wars. How many on this one little planet alone? Maybe 100,000? You know, universe, I think you set us up on this one, made us or our environment or both too inadequate so once in a while we went into grisly orgies of killing each other. for what? You don't know? Aren't you supposed to know? Aren't you supposed to contain all of us, our emotions, our dreams, our Gods, everything that has been created, ever created? What? I can't hear you. Our mementos on the clothesline, hung out as though to dry in the solar and ultimately stellar winds, no gravity out there, so things don't hang down, there's no down or up out there things hang down and hang up and frontwards and backwards. But mostly it's our essences out there on the clothesline, our dreams are strung out on it and since they came in our sleep maybe they never lived, or died, they just never were or always were, so when we're asleep dreaming or not, are we out there on the clothesline, hanging on or hovering nearby? Joey wants his sister's corsage from the junior prom hung out. Jerry wants his first poems written to girls in his adolescense to be recorded for immortality, because all his heart and soul went into them. Everything he ever knew he was went into them. Have you ever done more than that, universe, about anything? Eremius from the 11th century told me in a dream to get his poetry out. The poetry he thought but couldn't write becaue he lived in poverty in southern Italy and never learned to write. I want everyone to know what it's like to watch the setting full moon in the dim light of dawn on our planet, to watch a bluejay in the early morning chaparrel dart through the brush, to hear several different bird calls from a distance, to see graceful mountain ridges in that pale light above silky silent haze, to hear the city early morning hum, to show the pistol that ended my daughter's life with her own blood on her fingers and her lips, and to ask oneself what difference does it make, we all have to die sometime, ask the universe that hardly notices but let me say a death like this makes a great difference, an awful terrible difference. Once the word got out about this clothesline I've been hearing from - I mean millions, thousands, in my dreams and my imagination mostly only through me the things on the line now the sounds of the Virgin Mary's first orgasm. The dried up ear Vincent cut off, now a rich celestial source of self-regenerating gold and yellow pigment. Ruben's palette hanging on a clothespin, Rembrandt's, Goya's. Pope Urban the 4th's obscene poetry, Queen Elizabeth's thought-channeled correspondence with William Shakespeare. William the Conqueror's first tooth, Charlie Chaplin's unpublished published scripts - everything just everything my mother's sewing machine the old Victrola we played Von Suppe's Poet and Peasant overture on when I was 6 and could just peak over the top of and watch it turn. Bhutia Tensing's shoes he wore with Hilary when Tensing was the first human to scale Mt. Everest, everything everything, there's room for everything it's piling up from the ages wanting to be conscious real remembered knowing each other. And then - from the other end some tugging on the line from Andromeda. They - they? - want the same to endure as long as the unconscious universe endures but we have to participate with consciousness to tell it to be conscious, more conscious, and their signals flicker toward us along the thin filament between us that at any point along fades to nothing in the void in both directions, this filament that holds our memories our dreams and realities where all sensations are echos of themselves, held as though magnetically on this cosmic wire, this silver thread between our hearts and the hearts of Andromeda; rutile needles at the speed of light stabbing through the one great crystal of the universe, at a million times the speed of light, to relieve us of our yearning to be seen and shared, because these rutile needles bear our lives, our love of life, the wish for life a force a billion times greater than even light's love for itself with its origins in boiling stars. And now other galaxies have noticed our great loving clothesline with Andromeda and all that vast life everywhere wants to be forever the same as long as stars last - without end and so let all the kisses in the universe be remembered for the little meteors they were on one dear planet or between two separate worlds where kisses can be felt. So, from any planet to look out at the great gorgeous sculptured figure of life, shimmering and flexing with its own gratitude for the part that we who live have played and given the universe itself a living immortality. Then all the universe is lit by silver threads like parabolic elliptical sculptures, the trace of the infinite number of lines from each galaxy to every other, all these giving us the shape of life, the sight of life, of memory, of desire of time and space at last existing as a shimmering sculpture of dreams and laughter of sadness and tears of holding each other through all eternity and through the infinite space of our endless universe, now full alive with all our selves. With love and memory's immortality and even the universe that is lifeless over vast reaches yet did allow life to have its magic in the streaks of lightning on all the earths, the great hyperbolic parabolic shapes entwined with every galaxy joining everything across time and space at last. the whole universe now visible as our child the universe itself the child of our love of our capacity for love the greatest force of all after all!
