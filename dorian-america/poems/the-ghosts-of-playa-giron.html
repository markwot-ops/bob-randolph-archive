The ghosts of Playa Giron

Remember - Primitivo, Jose, Carlos, Crecencio, Dagoberto and Raul . . . it was hot hot that morning - as it always is at Playa Giron and you were worried, there was news of bombs at Ciudad Libertad and Santiago our planes - our 9 planes, the radio crackles, but something's at the coast - here at Playa Giron, los gusanos, the gusanos! down the narrow roads in little trucks it is always hot, your guns and gear your heart pounding your hands chilled in the heat, They came ashore at 4 AM, it's 9 AM now. It's on us, the militia. The Army - where are they? It's on us! My heart is pounding. I hear guns ahead, mortars, and then - the cottages, the beach beyond get on the ground, off the road! Hide! the cottages, a machine gun by the dining hall, 2 dead floating in the pool bloody streaks theirs? ours? Get low, get back, hide! There they are. Fire! Crash! Darkness. Hot place on my temple, burning sensation, My vision is dark gray. I hear faint voices. I hear no voices. It is dark D a r k. D a r D a Primitivo! Oh, Primitivo! Run! Get back! Julio, Crecencio, Dagoberto, Rufino, Pedro, Raul get back! The Army came, finally at 1 PM, to help us out. Fidel is here! Fidel! It is midnignt. Oh, god think of those of us who are lost. Primitivo, from my town. . . . We are exhausted. We need sleep, someone sings Adelante, Cubano - softly, in the night. The pale light of dawn. Pink clouds. Rifle fire before dawn. Smoke - explosions, groans and blood. We're gaining. It is hot again. Our wounded we are taking prisoners. Look at them! U.S. Army clothes. Unsmiling, looking at the ground, limping, hot day at Playa Giron. Always hot. U.S. weapons. U.S. ships. We sank the Houston, I hear. Never heard of it. Big ship, they say. Smoke and sweat and piss. More prisoners - always hot. Our armor moves up. We're taking it back. Almost over. Hot, always hot. Drive these gusanos into the sea; this filth that violates us. These sons of the rich, sons of the privileged, these sons of landowners, these batistianos, these murderers of our dreams, of our lives, these scum . . . And so I learned the story of Primitivo and the others - who died. and I had lived a day - an invitado - at Playa Giron - a month before their deaths. One month! before Primitivo died, and his vision grew dark in the heat, and all sound softened into silence in his ears, as it did for Julio and Crecencio, Dagoberto, Rufino Pedro and Raul, for the beast they fought is the same I fight and fear and despise and am in the belly of - and would chain once and for all in history's museum of memories and so, Primitivo and Raul and Pedro and all the others of the 156 who died, we have different homelands but I tell you myself that you fought for me and for others around the world, this tiny world and its tiny history but that was large enough to encompass giant Playa Giron and its struggle against the dragon of the world, and your Apollo-like heroism, your Minerva-like committment to love and laughter, and children in the park and babies smiling on our laps. Thank you deeply Primitivo and Crecencio and Dagoberto and Raul and Enrique, those two hot days gave you forever a place of memory in ourselves and in the memories of our hemisphere - and we will tell your story every time we fear and are in doubt, the story of those days of long ago, of 30 years ago, those courageous days, those hot days of April 1961.
