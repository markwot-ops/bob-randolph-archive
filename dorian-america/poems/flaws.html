Flaws

An emerald's beauty comes from flaws, a hint of something gone awry in all the axes of its gorgeous green. Amethyst, crystal-faced with lavendar light makes quartz more than itself. All so because distemper taints a rocky little soul deep down within itself. Disordered atoms give a gentle smoke-like hue to simple quartz— so richly seen because it's tinged with pain. The topaz, yellow, brown translucent light, so wrought by form that twists and writhes before our eyes; so pure an amber flash that weeps within itself. come to light as ruby— or sapphire, to yield at last such god-like rose or blue, so softly starred upon our hand. The diamond's poverty of color— its soul is only on a cutting edge, where sudden light cries out in blue and red and mauve… to tell us more of broken hopes, of laughter stabbing on a knife-like edge to ease some inner cry. All these are teachers for us now So fixed in time and space, their beauty shaped in fever heat— locked in a longer time than we. January, 1987
