A Revolution is..... Nicaragua is......

It is a boy who looks 6 but says he's l0 Nicaragua is brown Maya faces in repose, and can read my watch. A revolution is a skinny horse, It is two 20-year-olds in fatigues that return a smile easily, clear eyes, lips steady and full, rifles slung carelessly. Nicaragua is a crowded bus with 3 kids on the back and 6 on top. A revolution is 5 children staring at you, It is 3 pigs crossing the road but we miss them. It is a 45-year-old pregnant wife at a country stop. Nicaragua is a dark rustic store with one light bulb; It is bullet holes in the Bella Peluquería, the Supermercado around the corner, in the Santa Victoria Catholic church, the Union Ferretería, the city hall and Senora Guiterrez' Panadería. The revolution is gray patch marks on the city hall from the day the national guard shot it up eight years ago. A revolution is good guys and bad guys and sometimes you couldn't tell the difference. It is a gravel road with potholes where before there was no road at all. It is 9 mothers in front of the public building with pictures of their missing sons and daughters, missing since long before the revolution. Nicaragua is a cross on the highway where Jaime didn't see the truck coming. The revolution is running water in the village for the first time. It's old Miguel laughing a toothless laugh when Maria tries to teach him to spell "casa". The revolution is pulling guard duty from midnight to dawn so the contras can't dynamite the rice storage; It's pulling guard duty so the contras won't kill the volunteers picking coffee; It's pulling guard duty so maybe you can ward off air or sea strikes by the CIA on the oil storage docks; It's pulling guard duty at the bridge they blew up last April that was fixed within a week; It's pulling guard duty when there is more useful work to be done; It's getting tired of pulling guard duty but you know you have to. The revolution is no kids starving - anywhere. It's being able to buy school clothes for the children. It's a new school for the village or the barrio, It's a new health clinic for the village or the barrio. Nicaragua is l7 people in the back of a pickup truck. It's a beloved revolutionary priest who lives and stands with the poor. It's people standing on the highway even in the dark waiting for a bus that won't have room for them but they'll get on anyway. Nicaragua is painting things different colors just for the hell of it. It's the church mural of Jesus as an Indian youth crucified in hands and feet and stabbed in the side. It's young people running the country and making mistakes and what did you expect - it was all so new and enemies were on every hand bought and paid for by the U.S. that knew everything and knew nothing needed changing. It's 3 guys pushing a car that won't start. Nicaragua is everyone returning your smile - everyone. Nicaragua is the U.S. having to run everything in Latin America - in its own "national interest". Nicaragua is some East German trucks because they needed some kind of trucks and the U.S. had embargoed this poverty-stricken country because it was Marxist or something. It's Antonio with his kids in the army truck he's driving to Corinto. It's armed campesinos that can't even spell socialism or capitalism. It's salsa dancing in every barrio on Saturday night. Nicaragua is nobody with much money, but plenty of friends. The revolution was 50,000 who died fighting the National Guard. The revolution is kids with balloons filling the front seat of the army truck they had to buy half way around the world, though the balloons and some bubblegum were smuggled through the U.S. embargo. Nicaragua is Raoul complaining about his kidneys and his low wages - maybe a good urologist could give him a revolutionary attitude. It's 2 or 3 beggars in Leon instead of two or three hundred in Leon back under Somoza. Nicaragua is having the money for a service and a party for the whole barrio to remember "Abuela" Magdalena at the time of her death; we all knew the unbelievable chances she took to get food to the rebels in the war against Somoza. Nicaragua is bongo drums, guitars, and a marimba at a mass, touching both the Catholic God and the Revolution. It's having a TV set on your block and something worth watching on it. The revolution is being able to make love in privacy in your own little house. It's being able to question the doctor because you know a little something too. The "Revolution" is being in "Nicaragua", both the way it is and the way it was. The Revolution is being... The Revolution is being proud... proud that you are a "Nicaraguan", grateful that you're there to live out the achievements of your people, to have more time to laugh, to sing, to grow old, with those you love, and with those you have learned to love....
