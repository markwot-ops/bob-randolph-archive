Wounded knee

was an intersection between two worlds, two geologic epochs, two lightning storms, two rivers, two tectonic plates in human history, two men, one wise native chief, one illiterate white man. It ended the last community of Lakota in the wilds, the last 200 women and children and a few warriors. The great geologic divide between open plains, steep crags in the alpine glow at sundown, fish leaping in rapids to spawn, gently-grazing horses of the Sioux, the Lakota, and on the other side, the complete surrender of life to machinery, to electricity, to roaring engines and smashing horrifying drop hammers. The divide is across a gulf, a lacuna, that mankind had to create a route across, a fracturing of all calendars and rosetta stones. Wounded Knee was between the Paleozoic and Mezozoic eras in human history, between simple processes beside rivers and in the shade of fruit-bearingf trees, and the huge iron-clad rectangular-scaled reptiles, showing how progress can be hideous. Wounded Knee occurred between two lightning storms, during the inky blackness between bolts, so living creatures would not see the ugliness of power, of the consequences flaming in the night, and scorching the grasslands where the buffalo stood in herds, and native children looked toward the mountains with wonder. Wounded Knee was healed in former years by its caressing in river waters, to excite circulation in icy winters, to breathe fresh life in warmer summer water. Wounded Knee is a symbol for all the invasions of the white man, bringing with him all the maladies of modern life, including rifles to kill those of different colors. Tectonic plates merged on the eastern slopes of the great mountain chain that wrinkled up a hundred million years ago, that showed across the plains the beauty of snowy crags, for all the tribes to be guided by, toward places of safety throughout the seasons. Yet the tragic end was born of a redneck Indian agent, who knew only anger and fear and ignorance, and who wired Washington that the sacred ghost dances of the Lakota were war dances and had to be crushed, stamped out, killed if necessary, to we could go west across the horizon where millions of acres of mineral ores and buffalo and ownership of land, ownership of forests and mountains, and peaks and glaciers and deserts and wild life we could hunt down and slaughter for furs and redneck trophies, all in the service of beautiful murderous conquest -- all this became Wounded Knee in our history, that someday through magic and wildlife dreams and the whispers of clouds and the murmer of cold mountain streams will be seen as the hideous murders of Lakota of every age, even babies in their papoose carriers, but for now, the story must be mistold, for at least two centuries, so we can get on with murders of villages, cities, neighborhoods and farmland and wild deer and wild salmon and wild spirits of the dead of our own kind, before World Wars III and IV and World War V have come and gone, long before the sun burns itself out. . . .
