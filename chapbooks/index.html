Chapbooks

A chapbook is a small, inexpensive booklet — usually under 50 pages, folded and stapled at the spine and built around a single theme or a connected run of poems. The name comes from “chapman,” the old word for the traveling peddler who once sold them door to door. Popular across Europe from the 16th to the 18th centuries — ballads, folklore, religious tracts — the form was revived in the 20th century by the American Beat poets to get counterculture writing into readers' hands quickly and cheaply. It is the way Bob published his own work.
