50 bullets in a Persian poet

He dared to see a red sunset and call it red. He dared to view the stars and wonder what their fire was. He dared to see a woman's veil and wonder why. He dared to wonder about the secret police. He wondered why he and his beloved could not touch in public. Who said it should be so? Why was his friend Feras hanged from a bridge girder for protesting unemployment in the square? Why, oh why? He wondered, and wrote and published. But one night, they came and took him, and tortured him, and at dawn placed many bullets in him, then more and more, til 50 bullet holes taught lessons to others viewing his rotting body tied just there, beneath his poetry nailed above, with bullets through his words. But many hesitated, to read those words - furtively, and began to write fresh words, furtively, and look about to see who could be trusted to share them - furtively. And so, here I am, without bullets in my chest or face or arms, with a memoriam of words for a Persian poet of my own time, . . . , who so eloquently - speaks to us from his 50 tiny mouths.
