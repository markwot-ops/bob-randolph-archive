Manzanita

You wild dancers, you sinewy snakes weaving with intoxicated curiosity to see just who's around in this thousandth year of yours. Your dead wood, so firm like agate, with the lines of life of rhythms, cycles in your growth like the agate like jasper chalcedony and chert who all seem silent but are merely pictures at rest for the moment we gaze at them. These manzanita sisters, dancing wild sensuous lives on hot dry slopes. You manzanita your silky maroon forearms, your silky soft thighs, your vaginas where your limbs combine you devils that now at last can bring sanity to our frozen lives to free us to stir our bodies, minds and spirits together like a swirling batter for a wondrous marble cake of consciousness. Oh, manzanita, you manzanita sisters, stay with us, and if you dance away at night or just at dawn, remember us and know that we too have now absorbed your hardy love of life, that we too will flourish even with but 10 drops of water. We will dance all night to ancient songs both yours and ours and you and we, will think of one another at night and in the day and know our buds and seeds will spring anew to life on any slope, to tell the world and air to stay and see the sacred dance of you and me that only comes to rest when someone gazes in the eyes of you, a manzanita tree, a tree designed to help us humans see and be, with deep-set roots - like you, a manzanita tree.
