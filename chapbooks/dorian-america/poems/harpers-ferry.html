Harper's Ferry

The remains of this gap in the Blue Ridge Mountains, 2 mysterious rivers, the Shenandoah and the Potomac, with slanted sheets of rock in their rapids, between black cliffs. The body of John Brown still hangs from his gallows at this place, who fought slavery too soon, who seized this place, this arsenal of guns and cannons too soon, with too few men -- to be the signal for revolts by slaves before they learned to read. This intersection of all the fates of earth, of life and death, of million-year-old cataclysms that took place before man learned of this dread place, this Harper's Ferry. This long dead python snake Chesapeake Canal since 1824, in disuse since 1924, with these rivers and cliffs like sentinels watching colliding armies, this carving out with knives and guns the illness of a nation built on living death for Africans for 300 years. This place, this awesome place of historic, dreadful worlds . . . . This Harper's Ferry . . . .
