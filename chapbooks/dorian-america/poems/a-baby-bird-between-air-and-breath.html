A baby bird between air and breath

Not every life is lived. Some are hatched and live naked in the nest for a few days, learning how to hold its little head up and desperately learning how to open its little beak, its tiny yellow beak, its upper and lower beak it must learn to open just hours out of the egg. Just hours, and the world is huge and very cool and the sky is huge and the most precious thing this little bird owns is its beak -- for that is how it will have a chance to live. And so all these babies that are not yet fully babies, but barely dwell between air and breath, between gentle rain at night and the beginning of blood between worlds. And on that day this tiny spirit rested in my hand, this baby bird, naked with its lovely perfect two-lipped beak in its little body that flickered on because two parent birds mated, and flickered out because a tiny catastrophe pushed it over the edge in a heart-breaking instant. During the remaining 20 years of mine, I will remember this darling whose life ended when it began, whose love ended, whose dream of life ended -- and is now in me, to tell its story in all the seasons, in all the phases of the moon in all the chapels, all the gardens, and on all the windy slopes where life struggles to survive -- for a while.
