← The Picture of Dorian America · poem 45 of 72 · BOOK III — I Am Writing of Handgrenades, Butterflies and Kisses

Guatemala

"To watch the sunrise after a night of fullness with you. To think that by the time the cock crows the last time at dusk, and the donkey brays at the heated night, the stars moistly glistening, by then? the new life in you will have become our dark- eyed child, to give us both the fragrance of our seed, our shared perfume, our pollinating night on earth alone-" So many lines of gentle imagery you wrote, my son. You showed them to us all, unsurely. Where did such lines begin? Where were their origins? Where is a poet's will? Today I saw something of the roots of words you wrote. Your concave wrists, all soaked in blood, the tendons pearly white that signalled to the pencil in your hand, to write your words on grimy sheets - sweaty, stained, your severed hand stinking in the grass, with pearly nubs and clotted flies, with all of you - your mutilated manhood, before they severed it and stuffed it in your mouth. Oh, my God! Even Satan never did this thing to anyone. What a fiendish hell this "native land" of ours has once again become, with black and bloody soil, made so by those we thought we knew, who do these deeds like men gone mad, like bounty hunters who never wrote a poem in their lives, who never read a poem in their lives, or did they? before this lunacy hid the sun and severed all ourselves...
