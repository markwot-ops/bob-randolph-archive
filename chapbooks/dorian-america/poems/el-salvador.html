El Salvador

Maria Conchita gave birth to her baby on the escape trail they all followed, then she stood up with it and followed the others. Each carried their worldly goods, most with a belt tied around and carried over the shoulder or under one arm. When they rested under a small tree on a level place on the mountain, she tried to nurse it but the milk was not ready, the baby was only 30 minutes old. The infant tried to cry but did not know how yet. After fitfull sleep under the whump whump whump of the helicopters they ate what little they had cold, then trudged on to the secret cave and under lantern light where Dr. Clement stayed. Thorn scratches, infected mosquito bites, abrasions from loose gravel - bandages were needed, but the soldiers were told possessors of bandage cloth or bandaids were communists and to protect El Salvador needed to be destroyed. Usually just one slug, then dragged to the edge of town or the edge of the trail to stiffen in death in the blazing sun. So they started to use mosquito netting where they could find or steal it, and then those with mosquito netting began to disappear, and sometimes would be found in the weeds in the hills a week or two later when the stench of death reached the village. So then somehow they got diapers, anywhere, clean cloth for bandages, and so Maria Conchita, as she left a bus with her baby and a package of 5 diapers and she did not have the stamp mark on her I.D. paper she kept in her sweaty shoe, so they knew she was a rebel and they arrested her and screams behind the bus, then a shot and she was silent and only the crying of the baby was heard, and then there was another shot, and then there was silence. Dr. Clement estimated 40,000 had died in El Salvador in his 3 years there and the helicopters with machine guns and the tiny spotter planes with rockets could glide low and fire into any group of quiet thin men and women and children and babies would be killed because only rebels were in the hills like that. And if they tended their crops, they knew they were rebels, because everyone else had left their land and gone into the cities. If you stayed on your land, it meant that you supported the rebel army in the hills, so you should be killed to help save the country. At least that's what the military said the U.S. advisers told them, while all this time Americans were going to football games on Saturdays in the United States and to church on Sunday and were proud to be who they were, although they could never save any money because of big hospital bills and the children in big programs that cost a lot of money, and most thought El Salvador was the name of a church in Mexico or somewhere, and that was all, and the El Salvador generals were rich and knew a lot of Americans. But, but . . . , but . . , none of this was terrorism, it was all just fighting the enemies of the free world.
