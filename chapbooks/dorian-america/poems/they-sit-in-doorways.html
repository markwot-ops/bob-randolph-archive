They sit in doorways

They sit in doorways in the shady heat, in La Habana, Sao Paolo, Buenos Aires and New Orleans on hot afternoons in these cities born in the centuries. The dogs wander with mange, their ribs, their desperate faces, with stumbling steps seeking even drops of spit to lick. And yet there is great machinery to carve out factories, opera houses, marbled mansions and government buildings. And yet - they sit in doorways in the heat, the shady heat, their appealing empty faces looking our way. The dogs wander with their mange, their lost looks, their desperate steps, to find anything to lick. We have built empires, fought complete world wars, destroyed cathedrals, killed ten million babies on two continents, but they sit in doorways in the heat, the shady heat, watching us with hope and without hope. The dogs wander in the heat, their sore feet, their desperate hunger, they wander in these cities of their hell, in these cities of neglect. We send satellites to Saturn, invade tiny countries, medium countries, very tiny countries, we threaten big countries, we undermine medium large countries, we help two corporate giants fornicate and give birth to one hydraheaded cosmic corporation. They sit in doorways in the heat, the shady heat, watching us. The dogs wander in the heat, their desperate ribs, their barren furry faces, looking for a bit of food, a tiny bit of love, a tiny bit of earth, to finally sanctify those heated pavements, and those shady doorways, where they sit, here in the tropics, in the heat.
